Infosys / TCS pair (paper)

Daily bars. When Infosys and TCS drift unusually far apart, bet on them coming back together: the gap is ln(INFY close) − ln(TCS close), measured against its own last {{ infy_tcs_stats.window or 60 }} days (z-score). At a close with |z| ≥ {{ infy_tcs_stats.entry_z or 2 }}, enter at the next day's open with equal rupees on each leg — short the rich one, long the cheap one. Exit at the next open once z has crossed back through its average, or after {{ infy_tcs_stats.max_hold or 20 }} trading days. No stop-loss, one position at a time. ₹{{ "{:,.0f}".format(infy_tcs_stats.notional or 1000000) }} on each leg; costs {{ infy_tcs_stats.cost_pct or 0.2 }}% a round trip.

Evidence status: a lead, not a proven edge. Walk-forward on three years of daily data, the test period (January 2025 – October 2026) made +22% per rupee of one leg in 12 trades, 8 of them winners. But the 15 months before it lost 7–9%, all the profit came from seven consecutive winners, and over the full three years the average trade is not distinguishable from zero. The book is filled back to {{ infy_tcs_stats.paper_start or '2026-06-01' }}; sessions from {{ infy_tcs_stats.first_unseen or '2026-10-12' }} are the first the research had not seen. Not modelled: this uses cash share prices and fractional shares, while a real version needs stock futures on both legs (lot sizes, basis and roll costs); and it is not confirmed whether the daily candles are adjusted for dividends — an ex-dividend drop would look like a move in the gap.
