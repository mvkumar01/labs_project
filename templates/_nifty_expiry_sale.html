NIFTY expiry-day straddle sale (paper)

On every NIFTY expiry day — the day the nearest contract in the chain expires — the at-the-money straddle of that contract is sold in the morning and both legs are bought back at the first snapshot at or after 15:15. One lot ({{ nifty_expiry_sale_stats.qty or 65 }} units a leg). Three books: A sells at 09:45 at the strike nearest the index; B sells at 09:21 at the strike nearest the forward the chain implies; B50 is B with a stop on the pair at 50% (both legs bought back at the first snapshot that shows the two together half as dear again as they were sold for).

Evidence status: a lead, not a tested edge. It was found by the Strategy Tester on 46 expiry days (July 2025 to 18 August 2026); the rows tagged “in the research sample” are the eight of those on or after 1 June 2026. Rows tagged “not seen in research” and everything from {{ nifty_expiry_sale_stats.live_from or '2026-10-10' }} on are the new evidence. Nothing here is tuned from what the book shows. Fills: each leg is sold at its bid and bought back at its ask, then the short-option charges. “Research gross” is the same trade on middle prices before costs, in basis points of the index — the number to hold against the research. Selling a straddle needs margin and its bad days are large (research: worst day −77 bps on quotes, −321 bps on daily files). A day without usable quotes at the entry or the exit is skipped with the reason; no other time is substituted.
