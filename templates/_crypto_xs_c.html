Crypto taker imbalance · crypto only (paper)

The same rule as Crypto taker imbalance — each day at 00:00 UTC the {{ st.top or 50 }} most liquid contracts, ranked by (taker buys − taker sells) / volume over the last 24 hours, long the most bought and short the most sold, the average of the last {{ st.hold_days or 7 }} daily targets, traded at 01:00 UTC, {{ "%.0f"|format(st.cost_bps or 7) }} bps, funding — with one change: only crypto contracts. Binance's TradFi perpetuals (US, Hong Kong, Korean and Chinese stocks, ETFs including 3× leveraged ones, gold, silver, oil, FX) are left out before the volume ranking, so the top {{ st.top or 50 }} are the most liquid crypto contracts. Same quantity-based paper account, from ${{ "{:,.0f}".format(st.equity_start or 100000) }} on {{ st.paper_start or '2026-06-01' }}.

Why: by mid-2026 10 to 20 of the top 50 were TradFi contracts, which did not exist in the research's 2021–2024 discovery data; in the original book they lost about 10% of equity from June to mid-October. Which contracts count as crypto: Binance's own label (exchangeInfo underlyingType COIN), from a snapshot taken on a machine the Binance API answers (this server gets HTTP 451); a contract missing from the snapshot is crypto if it trades at least 35% as much on weekend days as on weekdays over the previous 28 days (stock and commodity contracts go quiet while their markets are shut). This twin was defined on 18 October 2026 after seeing the book to mid-October, so its June–October history is shown for context only; only decisions from {{ st.forward_from or '2026-10-19' }} are its test. A candidate being watched, not a proven edge.
